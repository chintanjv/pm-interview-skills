# Favorite Product Interview: SPARK + GUIDE

Use this skill when the user asks "What's your favorite product (and why)? How would you improve it?", or a variant: favorite app, website, physical product, AI product, a well-designed or badly designed product, or the last thing they bought. The user may name a product they actually use. They may also name a round (recruiter screen, hiring manager, loop, leadership) or a target company.

The framework was synthesized from:
- *Cracking the PM Interview* (McDowell & Bavaro), ch. 14, "Type 3: Favorite Product": goal, then what makes it neat, then alternatives, then improve.
- *Decode and Conquer* (Lewis Lin): ch. 1's design scorecard (up to 3 named criteria, backed by evidence and contrast) and ch. 2's CIRCLES method.
- About 13 web guides: Aced/Exponent, IGotAnOffer, RocketBlocks, Product Café, Crack FAANG, PM Interview Prep Club, Shipping Tomorrow, Product Gym, HelloPM, Product Alliance, PM Exercises, and others.

Core finding: almost every answer follows the same order (what it is, the problem, why it wins, the competitors, an improvement). Top answers stand out because they:
1. state a user goal, not features;
2. judge the product on 3 named criteria, with evidence for each;
3. give an owner-level insight (a deliberate trade-off, a growth loop, or a moat);
4. run the improvement as goal → segment → problem → one chosen solution → metrics.

## Mode selection
- **Answer mode (default):** produce an interview-ready answer in the user's voice.
- **Score mode:** the user pastes their own answer or a transcript. Score it with the checklist at the bottom, give the 3 highest-leverage fixes, and rewrite the weakest step.
- **Mock mode:** the user asks for a mock. Play the interviewer: ask the question, let the user answer one act at a time, push back with realistic follow-ups, then score.

## Before answering
1. **Product.**
    - **Named:** use it.
    - **Not named:** suggest 3 candidates that fit what you know about the user (role, domain, products they mention using), scored on the Step 0 tests. Ask which one they use, or proceed with the best fit and say so in one line.
2. **Round length.** Default to loop length (about 15 min spoken). If the user names a round, use the timing table.
3. **Target company.** If the named product belongs to the company's direct rival, say so in one line and suggest the user's backup product.
4. **Current features.** If web search is available, check the product's recent launches, so improvement ideas don't duplicate something that already ships. If an idea resembles an existing feature, say how it differs.
5. **Personal hooks.** Write the answer in the user's first-person voice. Use real details about the user only when you know them. Otherwise use clearly marked placeholders like [your moment]. Never invent personal history and present it as fact.

## Step 0: Product scorecard (when choosing or vetting a product)
The product should pass at least 7 of these 8 tests:
1. **Authentic:** the user really uses it and has a specific story about it.
2. **Explainable in one sentence.**
3. **Has a non-obvious "why"**: a design choice, trade-off or business model to unpack.
4. **Has a real gap** the user has felt.
5. **Known numbers:** rough scale, how it makes money, main competitors.
6. **Memorable:** not a default pick like iPhone, Instagram or Uber unless the angle is fresh.
7. **Safe:** not a direct rival of the target company, and not the user's current employer's product.
8. **On-brand:** shows the skills the target role needs.

Recommend a prep portfolio of products: a favorite, the target company's product, an AI product, a physical product, a recent purchase, and a poorly designed product.

## Answer mode output format
Use a heading per step with timestamps. Put the lines the candidate actually says in quotes, and use short bullets. Keep prose minimal. Be decisive.

Opening line, natural rather than recited: "My favorite product is X. I'll cover what it does for me, why I think it beats the alternatives, and then what I'd change."

### Act 1: SPARK, why you love it (about 4 min)
1. **S, Set the scene (0:00–0:30)**
    - Name the product and describe it in one line, as if to someone who has never seen it.
    - Say who it's for.
    - Give the user's concrete personal hook: a specific moment, not "I use it daily."
    - If the product is obscure, add a 15-second walkthrough of its core flow.
2. **P, Problem it solves (0:30–1:00)**
    - State the user's goal, not a feature. Example: "It doesn't track runs; it keeps me consistent."
    - Name 1–2 goals at most.
3. **A, Advantage: why it wins (1:00–2:30)**
    - Name 3 criteria upfront, then give one concrete piece of evidence for each.
    - Choose from:
        - Useful: does the job better.
        - Effortless: ease and design detail.
        - Innovative: a new approach.
        - Business model and flywheel.
        - Emotional: why people rave about it.
        - Honest and trustworthy.
4. **R, Rivals (2:30–3:15)**
    - Narrow: 1–2 direct competitors, and the one thing this product does better than each.
    - Broad: the substitute or old way.
    - Answer out loud: "Why don't I use X? Because…"
5. **K, Key insight: the owner's view (3:15–3:45)**
    - Give one non-obvious PM read: a deliberate trade-off (what they chose not to do, and why it was smart), the growth or engagement loop and the metric it likely optimizes, or the moat.
    - Then bridge: "Happy to get into what I'd improve."

### Act 2: GUIDE, how you'd improve it (about 11 min)
Open with: "Before ideas, let me pick what we're optimizing, for whom, and their biggest pain."
1. **G, Goal (0:00–1:30)**
    - State the company mission in one line.
    - Name the one goal the improvement serves: acquisition, activation, engagement, retention or monetization. Diagnose the product's biggest problem as an educated guess, then confirm it: "Does that match?"
    - Give a one-line reframe: "The problem isn't X, it's Y."
2. **U, Users (1:30–4:00)**
    - Name the ecosystem, then pick a side.
    - Name 3 segments by behavior or motivation, never demographics. They must not overlap.
    - Pick one with a "because" based on size, how underserved it is, and how much it moves the goal.
3. **I, Issues (4:00–7:00)**
    - Walk a 5–7 stage journey specific to that segment.
    - Name 3 distinct pain points phrased as obstacles, not features. Use no solution words yet.
    - Pick one on frequency × severity, state the root cause, and tie it to the goal.
4. **D, Design and decide (7:00–12:00)**
    - Give 3 genuinely different ideas:
        - A, quick fix.
        - B, leverage play using an asset only this company has.
        - C, bold bet, labeled as bold.
    - Avoid me-too ideas, "integrate X with Y" ideas, and features that already ship.
    - Pick one on impact × effort and name its main trade-off and mitigation.
    - Scope a v1: 3–5 must-haves, how users discover it, a narrow launch scope, and what's deliberately left out. Optionally make another idea v2.
5. **E, Evaluate and end (12:00–14:30)**
    - North star: the metric that captures delivered user value.
    - 2 supporting metrics.
    - Guardrails: metrics that must not get worse.
    - Top risk and its mitigation.
    - Validation: an A/B test or pilot with a pre-agreed bar. Never invent precise numbers.
6. **Close (14:30–15:00)**, 20–30 seconds: "For [segment] who struggle with [problem], I'd build [solution], which [how]. It fits because [company advantage]. I'd know it works if [north star] moves without hurting [guardrail]."

After the answer, add a short **"Before you use this"** list:
1. Which personal hooks to verify or swap in.
2. 2–3 sharp follow-ups this interviewer is likely to ask.
3. A reminder to check the product's changelog the week of the interview.

Then offer a mock.

## Timing by round
| Round | Length | SPARK | GUIDE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recruiter screen | 90 s – 2 min | S, P, and A (criteria one line each); R in one line | One improvement in one sentence, tied to real friction |
| Hiring manager | 5–8 min | Full | Goal → one segment → one pain → one idea + a metric |
| Loop, product sense | 20–30 min | Full (about 4 min) | Full (15–20 min), whiteboard columns Users, Issues, Ideas |
| Leadership | 3–5 min | S, P, K | The product's direction over 2–3 years, and why |

When the length is unclear, give the short version, then ask: "Want me to go deeper, or into how I'd improve it?"

## Rules
1. Every claim gets a "because" plus evidence. Adjectives without evidence ("clean", "intuitive") don't count.
2. Sound conversational. Never recite the framework's letters or steps to the interviewer.
3. Love the product and still name its biggest gap and its trade-offs.
4. Go narrow, then deep: one goal, one segment, one root problem, one chosen solution taken to a v1.
5. Protect the end. If running long, compress U and I, but never skip the pick, the v1 or the metrics.
6. Never say "I'd need more research" instead of making a decision.

## Score mode: 10-point check
Mark each item ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌ with a one-line reason.
1. The product passes the Step 0 tests and the hook is a specific personal moment.
2. P names a goal (1–2), not features.
3. A names 3 criteria, each with concrete evidence.
4. R covers narrow and broad alternatives, with a "why not them."
5. K gives an owner-level insight (trade-off, loop or moat).
6. G states the mission, a confirmed business goal, and a reframe.
7. U gives 3 behavioral segments that don't overlap, and picks one with a "because."
8. I gives 3 obstacle-phrased pains and picks one on frequency × severity with a root cause.
9. D gives 3 different ideas (one bold), picks one on impact × effort, and scopes a v1 with what's left out.
10. E gives a north star, a guardrail and a risk, then a 20–30 s close, and the answer fits the round's time.

Then give:
- a verdict: Strong, Borderline or Not yet;
- the 3 highest-leverage fixes;
- a rewrite of the weakest step.

Also flag the common failures:
- listing features (the iTunes story in *Cracking*);
- a cliché pick;
- no comparison to alternatives;
- pure fan mode with no flaw or trade-off;
- jumping to ideas before agreeing on a goal;
- many ideas and no pick;
- an idea that already exists;
- no metric;
- sounding scripted;
- running long.
